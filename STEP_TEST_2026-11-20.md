# ATOCIA — Conservative CPU step probe

Date: 2026-11-20. One CPU-bound Python worker was run for 20 seconds. RAPL and CPU package temperatures were sampled once per second. The probe has a 30-second maximum duration and stops at a measured CPU temperature of 75 C. No power limits, voltage, fan policy, kernel, or firmware settings were changed.

## Results

| Phase | Duration | Package mean | PSys mean | CPU package temp mean | CPU package temp max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Passive baseline | 30 s | 3.470 W | 9.730 W | 41.40 C | 43 C |
| One-worker probe | 20 s | 5.100 W | 12.270 W | 44.35 C | 47 C |
| Recovery observation | 30 s | 3.468 W | 11.305 W | 41.63 C | 42 C |

Package power rose by about 1.63 W (47%) during the probe and returned to the baseline range in the recovery capture. CPU package temperature peaked at 47 C and returned to at most 42 C during recovery.

PSys remained elevated during much of the recovery capture. This indicates other platform activity or background variation may have contributed; PSys must not be interpreted as CPU package power or added to it.

## Interpretation

This is a useful preliminary step-response observation: package power and temperature respond to a controlled short CPU activity pulse and return toward the prior range afterward. It is not yet a calibrated thermal model, proof of ATOCIA benefit, or evidence of energy savings. The experiment lacks measured ambient temperature, verified fan state, AC/battery state, and repeated trials. Sensor quantization and platform-specific zone meanings also limit precision.

## Next validation

Repeat at least three times under documented conditions, include a longer passive cool-down, measure ambient temperature, and compare a simple RC model against held-out samples. Only after the model is validated should a controller be evaluated, first in simulation/shadow mode without changing hardware power settings.
